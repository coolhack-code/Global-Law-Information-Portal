The Constitution of India ( Overview ):

The Constitution of India is the supreme law of India.

It defines how the country is governed, the rights of citizens, and the duties of the government.

It is the longest written constitution in the world.

When it was Adopted:

  • Adopted on: 26 November 1949
  • Came into effect: 26 January 1950

This is why 26 January is celebrated as Republic Day in India.

Who made the Constitution?

  • Written by the Constituent Assembly
  • Chairman of the Drafting Committee: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

He is also known as the architect of the Indian Constitution.

What Does the Constitution Contain?

  1. Preamble:

The Preamble explains the spirit of the constitution

It declares India as:

  • Sovereign
  • Socialist
  • Secular
  • Democratic
  • Republic

It promises:

  • Justice
  • Liberty
  • Equality
  • Fraternity

Articles of the Constitution

Article 1 - The Union and its Territory

  • India's official name is "India, that is Bharat".
  • India is a Union of States, not a federation that states can leave.
  • States and Union Territories together form India.

Article 2 - Admission or Establishment of New States

  • Parliament can add new states to India
  • This can happens if a new region becmes part of India.

Article 3 - Formation of New States

  • Parliament can:
    • Create a new state.
    • Change the name, area, or boundary of an existing state.
  • Example: creation of Telangana in 2014.

Article 4 - Laws Related to Articles 2 and 3

  • Any law made under Articles 2 and 3:
    • Does not require a constitutional amendment.
    • Can change state boundaries legally.

Article 5 - Citizenship at the Commencement of the Constitution

  • A person became an Indian citizen on 26 January 1950 if:
    • They were born in India, or.
    • One of their parents was born in India, or.
    • They lived in India for a certain period.

Article 6 - Citizenship of Migrants from Pakistan

  • People who migrated from Pakistan to India were given citizenship.
  • Conditions applied based on date of migration and registration.

Article 7 - Citizenship of Migrants to Pakistan

  • People who migrated from India to Pakistan:
    • Generally lost Indian citizenship.
    • Except those who later returned legally.

Article 8 - Citizenship of Indians Living Abroad

  • Indians living in other countries could become citizens if:
    • They registered themselves with Indian authorities

Article 9 - Voluntary Acquisition of Foreign Citizenship

  • If an Indian citizen voluntarily takes citizenship of another country:
    • They lose Indian citizenship.
  • India does not allow dual citizenship.

Article 10 - Continuance of Citizenship

  • Anyone recognized as a citizen:
    • Will continue to be a citizen.
    • As long as they follow Indian citizenship laws.

Article 11 - Power of Parliament on Citizenship

  • Parliament has the power to:
    • Make laws about citizenship.
    • Decide how citizenship can be acquired or lost.
  • This led to the Citizenship Act, 1955

Article 12 - Definition of the State

  • Explains what “State” means
  • Includes:
    • Central Government
    • State Governments
    • Local authorities
  • Fundamental Rights apply against the State.

Article 13 - Laws Inconsistent with Fundamental Rights

  • Any law that violates fundamental rights is invalid.
  • Protects citizens from unfairlaws.

Article 14 - Right to Equality

  • Everyone is equal before the law.
  • No discrimination by the state.
  • Law applied equally to all.

Article 15 - Prohibition of Discrimination

  • No discrimation base on:
    • Religion
    • Race
    • Caste
    • Sex
    • Place of birth
  • Allows special provisions for:
    • Women
    • Children
    • Backward classes

Article 16 - Equality of Opportunity in Public Employment

  • Equal Opportunity in government jobs.
  • No discrimination in public employment.
  • Reservation allowed for backward classes.

Article 17 - Abolition of Untouchability

  • Untouchability is completely abolished.
  • Practicing it is a punishable offense.
  • Ensures human dignity.

Article 18 - Abolition of Titles

  • The state cannot give titiles like:
    • Raja, Maharaja, etc.
  • Military and academic titles are allowed.
  • Promotes equality among citizens.

Article 19 - Right to Freedom

Citizens have freedom to:

  • Speak and express ideas.
  • Assemble peaceably.
  • Form associations.
  • Form associations.
  • Move freely.
  • Live anywhere in India.
  • Choose any Profession.

These freedom have reasonable restrictions.

Article 20 - Protection in Respect of Convictions

  • Protects people accused of crimes.
  • No punishment without law.
  • No double punishment for same offence.
  • No Forced confession.
